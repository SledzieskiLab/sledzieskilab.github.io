The vast majority of known functional data exists in human and a few of the best-studied model organisms. We have [previously shown](https://github.com/SledzieskiLab/D-SCRIPT) that models trained on human PPIs enable prediction of interactions in non-model species, and we have developed a [multi-stage workflow](https://github.com/SledzieskiLab/philharmonic) for detecting clusters of related functional proteins in diverse species. We are broadly interested in developing new methods for remote homology detection, molecular function annotation, and interaction network alignment and clustering.

![Bar chart comparing interaction counts across twelve organisms from BioGRID 2010 to 2023.](/assets/images/research/num_interactions.png)

We are currently seeking to expand the coverage of systems function in non-model organisms with the [Philharmonic Atlas](https://cb.csail.mit.edu/philharmonicDB/), and to partner with biologists for deep-dive investigations into discovering new molecular function using our lab's tools. We are particularly interested in studying membrane receptors, signaling pathways, and host-symbiont or host-pathogen interactions.
