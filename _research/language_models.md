Just as a revolution has occurred in natural language models from unsupervised representation learning on large corpuses of text, biomolecular foundation models trained on protein sequences and structures have emerged as a workhorse technology in molecular machine learning. The lab trains biological foundation models, and builds on them for predicting molecular properties ranging from structural dynamics to high-level function.

![Unsupervised learning on proteins leads to rich representations.](/assets/images/research/lm_example.png)

Specifically, we are focused on [accessible and efficient](https://accml.bio/) use of these foundation models. We have developed methods for [parameter-efficient fine-tuning](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2405840121) of protein langauge models, and consistently scaling model representations via [reverse distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07710).

![Reverse distillation of language model embeddings.](/assets/images/research/reverse_distillation.jpg)

The lab is currently pursuing advances in [multi-modal foundation models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24506), and seeks to develop new methods for pre-training and fine-tuning these models to address diverse biomolecular tasks.
